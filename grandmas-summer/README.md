# Grandma's Summer

**[sylvanus4.github.io/grandmas-summer](https://sylvanus4.github.io/grandmas-summer/)**

One summer day at your grandmother's house in the Korean countryside, 1996. You are sitting on
the 마루 with the door slid open onto the yard. Drag the sun across the sky and the whole day
moves with it: the light, the shadows, and every layer of the soundscape.

There is one control, and it is time.

---

## What it is

A single static page. No build step, no bundler, no framework, no dependencies, no network
requests after the first load. Plain HTML, CSS and ES modules.

The scene is entirely inline SVG and CSS. There is not one image file in the repository.

Every sound is generated by the Web Audio API at runtime. There are no `.mp3`, `.wav` or `.ogg`
files anywhere in this project, no sample packs and no licensed material. This is partly a
licensing decision and partly the interesting part of the build: a cicada is a shape you can
describe in maths, and once you can describe it you can make a hundred of them and set them
slightly out of phase with each other.

## How the sound is made

All of it lives in `assets/js/audio.js`. The top level graph:

```
cicadaNear ─┐
cicadaFar  ─┤                    ┌── dry ───────────────┐
fan        ─┤                    │                      │
chime      ─┼── per layer gain ──┤                      ├── sum ── highpass 42 Hz
birds      ─┤                    └── send ── convolver ─┘        └── compressor
crickets   ─┤                                (procedural IR)         └── master gain
tv         ─┤                                                             └── output
rain       ─┤
air        ─┘
```

Every layer gain is a continuous function of the hour of the day, so scrubbing the sun is a real
crossfade across the whole mix rather than a switch between presets.

### Cicadas (매미)

The layer that makes or breaks the piece. A Korean cicada is, acoustically, amplitude modulated
band limited noise, so that is exactly how it is built:

- a looping white noise `AudioBuffer` as the source
- two cascaded `bandpass` biquads at the same centre frequency, so the band is genuinely narrow
  rather than merely tilted
- a `sine` oscillator in the tens of hertz driving a `GainNode.gain` through a depth gain. That
  amplitude modulation is what turns a hiss into a buzz
- a second, slow (0.6 to 2.4 Hz) oscillator wobbling the bandpass frequency, so it does not sit
  perfectly still
- a call envelope written into the gain as scheduled ramps: a slow exponential attack, a sustain
  built from many small linear ramps between 0.74 and 1.06 of peak so it surges, and a release
  that stumbles down in five steps rather than fading politely
- the AM rate ramps up into the attack and falls away through the release, which is the thing
  that makes it sound alive rather than switched on

Two species run at once:

| | centre | AM rate | call length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 말매미 style | 3500 to 4100 Hz | 52 to 64 Hz | 4.5 to 11 s, the flat wall of sound |
| 참매미 style | 4900 to 5900 Hz | 30 to 40 Hz | 2.6 to 5.2 s, brighter, ragged tail |

Sixteen voices run permanently. Each has its own jittered centre frequency, AM rate, stereo
position and call timing, and seven of them are routed through a high shelf cut onto a wetter
reverb send so the yard has depth. The scheduler decides independently for each voice whether it
starts calling, weighted by the time of day. Peak density is around 14:00 and they are gone by
20:00.

### The fan (선풍기)

Brown noise (white integrated with a leak, written into a buffer) through a bandpass is the
airflow. Highpassed white on top is the hiss at the grill. A `triangle` at 61 Hz is the blade
passing frequency of three blades at about twenty revolutions per second, with a second harmonic
above it, and a quiet 120 Hz sine is the motor. One 0.055 Hz LFO drives the stereo pan, the
bandpass frequency and the level together, which is the head turning away and coming back.

### Wind chime (풍경)

Struck metal, so the partials are inharmonic: ratios 1, 2.76, 5.41 and 8.93, each a sine with its
own decay time from 2.9 s down to 0.66 s, plus a short bandpassed noise tick for the clapper.
A wind signal is computed as a slow sum of three sines, and strike probability is that gust value
raised to the 2.6, which is why the chime arrives in clusters rather than evenly.

### The rest

- **Birds**: two to five short swept notes, a single oscillator with `frequency` automation and
  12 ms attacks. Dawn only.
- **Crickets**: a triangle near 4.5 kHz through a narrow bandpass, pulse modulated at 21 to 32 Hz,
  gated into trills. Seven voices, staggered.
- **Frogs**: a sawtooth around 200 Hz through a formant bandpass, amplitude modulated at 13 to
  22 Hz, one to three croaks at a time.
- **Television in the other room**: band limited noise with a speech shaped envelope. Syllables of
  90 to 170 ms grouped into phrases, then a pause, with the formant filter jumping each syllable.
  Lowpassed at 1.5 kHz because you are hearing it through a wall. Nothing is intelligible, which
  is the point.
- **Shower**: lowpassed noise for the body and highpassed noise for the leaves, swelling over five
  seconds. While it rains, a duck gain drops the cicadas by 93 percent, and they come back
  slowly afterwards with a 2.4 second time constant.
- **Reverb**: a `ConvolverNode` whose impulse response is generated at startup into an
  `AudioBuffer`. 1.25 s, an 11 ms pre delay, three discrete early reflections off the wall and
  the floorboards, then noise under a `(1 - t)^2.6` decay, mildly lowpassed by averaging each
  sample with its predecessor so it does not sound like static.

Nothing runs on a timer per sound event. A single 170 ms scheduler with a 550 ms lookahead writes
all envelopes ahead of the audio clock, one shot voices disconnect themselves in `onended`, and
concurrent one shots are capped.

## The visuals

Time drives twenty two colour tokens through a twelve stop keyframe table, interpolated in JS and
written to CSS custom properties. Nothing switches; everything slides. Sun elevation and azimuth
are derived from the same variable and drive:

- shadow direction and length, as a per object `skewX` plus vertical `scale` about each object's
  own base point
- the parallelogram of daylight thrown through the doorway onto the floorboards
- shadow opacity, sky glow radius, bloom, and the warm overlay at both golden hours

Heat haze over the yard is an SVG `feTurbulence` into a `feDisplacementMap` whose scale follows a
midday curve and whose base frequency drifts slowly. Grain is a static `feTurbulence` tile in a
`multiply` blend, nudged on a stepped animation. Both stop under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`,
along with the fan and the laundry, while time scrubbing and all audio keep working.

## Accessibility

The sun is a `role="slider"` with `aria-valuemin/max/now/valuetext`, operable with drag, wheel,
touch and Arrow, Page, Home and End keys. The piece is meant to be comprehensible with the sound
off: the clock, a one line caption naming what you are hearing, and a live level meter per audio
layer are all on screen. UI text sits on opaque paper panels precisely so its contrast never
depends on how bright the sky happens to be at that moment.

---

## Running it locally

It is static, but it uses ES modules, so it needs to be served rather than opened from the file
system:

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8000
# then open localhost:8000 in a browser
```

Audio starts only after you press Begin, because browsers require a user gesture before an
`AudioContext` may run.
